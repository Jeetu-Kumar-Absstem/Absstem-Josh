## What Josh Does For Your Desk

Use Josh to control the procure-to-pay cycle from requirement and ordering through receipt, quality resolution, invoice validation, and vendor payment readiness.

## Process Flow

1. Requirement becomes Purchase Requisition.
2. Approved demand becomes Purchase Order.
3. Supplier delivery becomes Goods Receipt.
4. Quality exception becomes claim or purchase return if required.
5. Supplier bill becomes Purchase Invoice.
6. Three-way match validates PO vs GRN vs Invoice.
7. Vendor payment is released after commercial and control checks.

## How To Operate In Josh

1. Start by reviewing submitted requisitions, ageing purchase orders, inward gaps, and invoice exceptions that need management attention.
2. Use Purchase Requisitions and Purchase Orders to confirm priority, supplier commitment, and approval readiness before releasing work.
3. Check Goods Receipts, quality holds, and supplier claims in Josh before approving the next commercial or financial step.
4. Use 3-way Match, Vendor Statement, and Vendor Payments to resolve control gaps from one connected record trail.
5. Keep escalations, follow-up priorities, and closure status inside Josh so supplier issues do not split across calls, chats, and spreadsheets.

## How You Should Operate Daily

1. Review new and submitted requisitions.
2. Convert approved demand into POs.
3. Monitor undelivered or ageing POs.
4. Post inward receipts and review QC exceptions.
5. Validate purchase invoices and match exceptions.
6. Track payment-ready vendors and statement closure.

## What To Watch Carefully

- Watch for ageing requisitions, purchase orders, and invoice exceptions that indicate control drift.
- Check that inward, claims, and payment readiness are being resolved from connected Josh records rather than informal follow-up.
- Watch supplier escalations that keep bouncing between teams without a clear next owner in the system.
- Capture pilot feedback on approval flow, visibility, and control quality after real transactions move through Josh.
